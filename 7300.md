# 7300 · YAWRITE

> Spelled **YAWRITE** — a play on "yeah, right."
> **7300** = the number of days in 20 years (20 × 365).

A twenty-year commitment to living with intention every day, using **writing as
the anchor habit**. A Love Ando brand. Currently **Year 11 of 20 — entry 4101 of
7300**.

## Origin — the gap

Every personal-development teacher (Jim Rohn, Darren Hardy, Brendon Burchard)
*alluded* to writing or journaling as central to their growth — but none gave a
clear, systematic approach. They'd say "keep a journal," "write gratitude," and
then recommend **someone else's** system, never their own. That was a huge
oversight: if you're going to coach people on life, give them a system. So the
journey was to solve that problem personally.

## Purpose — what the anchor holds

This is not a productivity habit. It is **solid ground**, built when nothing else
stayed still.

Context: mother left at age two; father had custody and was physically and
verbally abusive, in and out of jail and rehab, eventually drowned in a river when
Ando was 11; from then, bounced around, very poor, lived with people from church.

The anchor provides two things:

1. **Solid ground** — one thing that is *for sure*, no matter where in the world
   you are or what's shifting around you. Everything else can be a variable; this
   you know. Stop, write, establish your thinking.
2. **Closure** — all you have to do is recap the previous day and accept the truth
   that **yesterday is over**. Agree with that reality. Don't go back and try to
   change it.

## The prompt structure

Each entry has a **header** written first:

```text
Saturday, August 2, 2026 — Your City, State — Entry 4101 of 7300

[the entry]
```

**The key move:** when you finish the morning's entry, you write **tomorrow's
header** immediately — the date, location, next entry number — and below it a dash
for "yesterday." So you wake up mid-momentum, never to a blank page or a decision.

Rationale: any question you have to answer ("what's the date? what entry am I
on?") is an opening to stop, check your phone, get distracted, or use fatigue as
an excuse not to write. Remove every question, remove every excuse.

## The trigger (keystone habit)

From *The Power of Habit* (Duhigg). The night before: set the journal and pen on
the counter, load the AeroPress, fill the kettle with water.

In the morning, the **first action is turning on the kettle** — which cascades
into the full routine: coffee, journaling, visualization, exercises.

## The payoff — 11 years in

- 11 years into the 20-year thesis; ~4,101 entries.
- **Never re-read the entries.** The value was living with intention, not the
  record.
- The one metric tracked: **consistency**. Missed entries logged honestly as
  "failed entry" in the column. Went from ~50% conversion in the first months to
  ~81% over 11 years — reframed not as loss but as proof of how much was captured.
- Foresight held: the 20-year timeline was built to absorb resets (losing your
  way, your mind, your wife, having to start over). Those exact storms came in the
  last 5 years, and the anchor held. It took ~5 years to figure out the "what," and
  the 10-year vision was **not** achieved at 10 — proof you must give yourself the
  time to figure it out, and time to fail at life outside your goals.

**Closing line:** *Time is a gift, if you welcome it into your life and treat it
as precious every day.* It compounds into the person you want to become and the
table you want to sit at.

## FAQs

- **What if someone reads my journal?** It's a real risk. Protect it — put it
  somewhere people won't find it.
- **Physical or digital?** Either. Writing physically was always the benefit, but
  it's hard when traveling (space). Methods tried: iPad journaling; a Moleskine
  smart pen + notebook (the pen was funky, uncomfortable); a reMarkable tablet
  (decent, but it glitched and reorganized all entries so they were lost); loose
  scratch paper ripped up for short entries (surprisingly good); and now
  **7300.io** — a daily voice record where AI pulls memories and turns them
  into archived generative-image artifacts, tagged like a photo packet.

## The mission

Open-source it. Not gatekept, not turned into an app to sell. Put the system as
markdown into a repo AI agents can grab for anyone whose ground is shifting.
There's a big gap in the world right now — people looking for an **anchored habit**
while the ground moves beneath them. Give them one clear thing to grab onto, analog
or digital, their choice. *Give away the thing that saved you.*
